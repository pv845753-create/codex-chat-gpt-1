# Your Attention Is Being Sold

Hardstart Films — original English screenplay.

## 01 — phone

Eli looked up from his phone just as the train doors closed. His station slid past the window. He had been holding the ticket for twenty minutes.

## 02 — train

Nothing urgent had happened on the screen. Someone had rearranged a bedroom. Someone had explained a controversy. A stranger had bought a car he would never drive.

## 03 — phone

Each item lasted a moment. Together, they had carried him across the city. He locked the phone and saw his own tired face in the dark glass.

## 04 — street

By the time he walked home, his dinner was cold. A message from his friend Leon waited beside a delivery advertisement. Still on for Saturday? He answered yes.

## 05 — desk

They had planned to cook together, something they used to do when their apartments were smaller and their evenings felt longer. Eli added it to his calendar.

## 06 — bed

Then he lay down for a minute. The phone rested above his face. One clip ended exactly where the next began, leaving no natural place to stop.

## 07 — kitchen

Saturday arrived. He bought tomatoes, bread, and a lemon. He imagined music in the kitchen, an unhurried meal, and a conversation that did not happen through messages.

## 08 — phone

While the kettle boiled, he opened the feed. A notification led to a comment. The comment led somewhere else. The kettle clicked off behind his shoulder.

## 09 — kitchen

Leon knocked twice before Eli heard him. They laughed about it. Eli placed the phone beside the chopping board, face up, close enough to check between slices.

## 10 — notes

Leon started telling him about a job interview. Halfway through, the screen lit up. Eli glanced down, nodded at the wrong moment, and asked a question Leon had already answered.

## 11 — mirror

There was a small silence. Not a fight. Leon looked at the tomatoes and said, I can tell you later. Eli had heard that sentence before.

## 12 — phone

He wanted to explain that he was listening. But he could not repeat what his friend had just said. The defense dissolved before it reached his mouth.

## 13 — diagram

On the screen, another item was always waiting. A cue invited a check. Checking revealed something new. Sometimes useful, sometimes empty. Either way, there was another cue.

## 14 — desk

The service did not know that this was his one free afternoon with Leon. It could count another interaction. It could not count the conversation he had missed.

## 15 — phone

An advertisement filled the display. He had not paid for the feed. Yet his afternoon had helped make its audience valuable to someone buying a place inside it.

## 16 — kitchen

Eli turned the phone over. Then it vibrated against the wooden counter. His hand moved before he decided to move it. He stopped with his fingers touching the case.

## 17 — phoneaway

He carried it into the hallway and put it in a drawer. There was nothing impressive about the gesture. He simply wanted the next choice to require standing up.

## 18 — kitchen

Back in the kitchen, he asked Leon to start again. Not because the interruption had never happened, but because it had. This time he put down the knife.

## 19 — notes

The interview had gone badly. Leon was embarrassed. Eli listened without collecting an answer in advance. There was no clever lesson, just a friend who needed a few minutes.

## 20 — water

They burned the first piece of bread. The smoke alarm complained. Leon laughed, then Eli laughed too. It was the first thing that afternoon he knew he would remember.

## 21 — phone

After dinner, he opened the drawer. Several notifications had accumulated. Most could have waited. One message mattered. He answered it and left the rest for later.

## 22 — desk

That night, he checked which alerts he actually wanted. Messages from people stayed. Promotional reminders went. He was not deleting modern life. He was making its entrances less noisy.

## 23 — diagram

He drew a simple boundary for ordinary evenings: choose a reason, use the tool, leave when the reason is finished. A boundary, not a promise never to get distracted.

## 24 — clock

The next morning, he reached for the empty bedside table. His phone was charging across the room. For a moment, he did not know what to do with his hand.

## 25 — window

He opened the curtain. Rain had stopped during the night. Water trembled along the railing. He had lived beside that view for years without noticing how the drops gathered.

## 26 — train

On Monday, he took the same train. He checked the route, answered a message, and put the phone away before the carriage reached the tunnel.

## 27 — phone

At lunch, he slipped back into scrolling and stayed longer than he meant to. He noticed the time, felt annoyed, and resisted turning one lapse into a verdict about himself.

## 28 — notes

He wrote down where it happened: tired, hungry, waiting alone. That told him more than calling himself weak. Next time, he could eat before opening the feed.

## 29 — bench

He still enjoyed things he found online. Some made him laugh. Some taught him something. The difference was whether he had chosen the visit or merely followed the nearest light.

## 30 — street

A week later, Leon sent a photograph of another questionable loaf. Eli smiled, called him, and made plans. The phone was useful. That had never been the enemy.

## 31 — window

His attention was not limitless. Spending it in one place meant being absent from another. He could not recover every lost hour, but he could decide where this one went.

## 32 — door

That evening, he stepped outside without reaching for the drawer. The city had no automatic next item. He had to choose a direction, and then look where he was going.

## 33 — end

Someone will always have another reason for you to stay. Before you give them the rest of your evening, ask who or what you meant to be present for.
