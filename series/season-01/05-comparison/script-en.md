# Someone Else’s Life

Hardstart Films — English narration script

## Beat 01 — train

The first photograph was not impressive. A wet bus stop. A crooked blue sign. His grandmother recognized it immediately. That is where we used to wait, she said.

## Beat 02 — books

He decided to make her a small book of their old neighborhood. Not famous places. The bakery window, the footbridge, the bench where she always stopped to rest.

## Beat 03 — phone

He opened an account for inspiration. A photographer stood above a mountain. Another leaned from a helicopter over a city he had never visited.

## Beat 04 — mirror

He looked down at his own picture. The blue sign suddenly seemed cheap. The rain looked gray instead of dramatic. Nothing in the photograph had changed except the comparison.

## Beat 05 — street

On his next walk, he passed the bakery without taking a picture. He was waiting for better light now. Something cinematic. Something that would belong beside those other photographs.

## Beat 06 — notes

He listed what serious photographers seemed to have: better cameras, better clothes, more interesting mornings. At the bottom, he had forgotten the book.

## Beat 07 — phone

He studied their rooms as carefully as their pictures. Their work seemed to come from a life that had already been arranged correctly.

## Beat 08 — window

His window faced a brick wall and a clothesline. He pulled the curtain across it. Then he spent the evening looking at someone else's view through a smaller rectangle.

## Beat 09 — desk

The camera stayed on his desk. He renamed his account and deleted the bus stop picture. It no longer matched the person he wanted to appear to be.

## Beat 10 — train

On the train, every dark window became a mirror. He checked his reflection, then a stranger's photograph, then his reflection again. He was measuring two things that did not belong together.

## Beat 11 — diagram

He had borrowed a scorecard without noticing. Their finished highlight against his entire ordinary day. Their chosen frame against everything he knew about himself. The comparison could never be fair.

## Beat 12 — mirror

Knowing the comparison was unfair did not immediately change how he felt. The camera gathered dust while his taste grew more demanding.

## Beat 13 — phone

His grandmother called on Sunday. Had he taken any new pictures? He said he was working on the idea. She asked whether the bakery still put bread in the window.

## Beat 14 — kitchen

He could not remember. He lived three streets away, but he had spent more time studying a mountain on another continent than noticing what happened outside his own door.

## Beat 15 — street

The next morning, a handwritten notice appeared on the bakery glass. Closing next month. The owner was retiring. There would not be an unlimited number of ordinary mornings after all.

## Beat 16 — door

He almost walked past. Then the owner stepped outside with a tray and asked whether he wanted anything. He stopped and bought one roll.

## Beat 17 — bench

He bought one roll and sat across the street. Steam clouded the paper bag. The sign leaned slightly to the left. He knew that tilt. His grandmother would know it too.

## Beat 18 — notes

He wrote a different question in his notebook. Not, would a stranger admire this? Would someone who knows this place recognize what matters?

## Beat 19 — diagram

This was the frame he could choose: his reason for making the book, one honest photograph, and the person he meant to share it with. A direction, not a popularity forecast.

## Beat 20 — street

He raised the camera. A bus interrupted the first shot. In the next, the owner laughed at a customer beyond the edge.

## Beat 21 — desk

The picture was uneven. He straightened it a little, then stopped. The laugh mattered more than the perfect angle. He wrote the date underneath and kept the photograph.

## Beat 22 — phoneaway

When he opened the reference account that evening, the mountain was still beautiful. He saved it without turning it into a verdict. Admiration did not have to become an instruction to disappear.

## Beat 23 — street

Over the next week, he returned to the footbridge. The railings had been repainted. He took a close photograph of a small patch where the old blue still showed through.

## Beat 24 — bench

At the bench, he waited until the rain stopped. Not for a dramatic sky. He wanted the wet wood and the empty space beside it, the place she usually put her bag.

## Beat 25 — train

He photographed the bus stop again. This time, he included the crooked sign. He could imagine his grandmother pointing at it before she read a single word of the caption.

## Beat 26 — books

Some pictures did not work. One was blurred. Another said nothing. He left them out. His own purpose still needed choices and care.

## Beat 27 — desk

He asked a friend to look through the pages. She suggested moving the bakery photograph earlier. He listened. A personal standard could still make room for useful eyes besides his own.

## Beat 28 — phone

One night, he slipped back into comparing. A younger photographer had won an award. He stared until his small book felt ridiculous. Then he remembered who was waiting for it.

## Beat 29 — books

He closed the account and printed the next page. It was an ordinary choice, and he would need to make it again. One evening of clarity had not ended the habit.

## Beat 30 — write

On the last page, he wrote: Some things changed. Some things stayed. I thought you might like to see. He left that sentence alone.

## Beat 31 — send

He mailed the book in a plain envelope. No public announcement. No promise that it would change anyone's life. Just twelve photographs that had finally reached the person they were for.

## Beat 32 — phone

His grandmother called and began with the crooked sign. Then she asked about the bakery owner. They talked for an hour.

## Beat 33 — window

Later, he opened his curtain. The brick wall was still there. So was the clothesline. A yellow shirt moved in the wind. For the first time in weeks, he reached for the camera.

## Beat 34 — end

Someone else's life can be beautiful without making yours a mistake. Before you change everything to fit their frame, ask what you wanted to see through your own.
